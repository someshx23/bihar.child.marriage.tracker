How to read this

Gap vs structure. A regression on 705 districts predicts the child-marriage rate from women's schooling (10+ years), improved sanitation and clean cooking fuel (R² = 0.43). Bihar's districts sit about 10 points above what that relationship predicts; the gap shown here is each district's distance from that Bihar average, so it separates districts whose rate is explained by weak schooling and poverty from those where something else is at work.

  • A. Rising (district or division, 90% level) or well above the structural expectation: campaign, enforcement and community norm work.
  • B. Above the state average but in line with its schooling and wealth, no clear trend: keeping girls in secondary school is the lever.
  • C. A clear fall, still above average: document what worked and keep it going.
  • D. Below the state average: maintain, and use as peer examples.

Uncertainty. Change bands are approximate 90% intervals from the reported sample sizes, allowing for survey design. Most single-district changes are within noise; only Bhagalpur, Purnia and Kishanganj rise clearly, and Gaya, Kaimur and Sheohar fall clearly.

Sources. IIPS, NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 district factsheets (rchiips.org/nfhs); compiled CSV verified value-by-value against the 38 official Bihar PDFs. Code, data and this page are in the project annexure.

The 20-24 cohort in NFHS-5 was mostly married before the October 2017 campaign began, so this survey can only show the starting line, not the campaign's effect. NFHS-6 will be the first real test.
